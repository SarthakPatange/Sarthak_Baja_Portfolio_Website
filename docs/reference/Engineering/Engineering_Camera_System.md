# Engineering Camera System

Version: 1.0

Status: Repository Standard

Purpose

The camera is the narrator of every engineering experience.

Visitors should never feel like they are operating CAD software.

Instead, they should feel as though an experienced engineer is guiding them through the product.

Camera movement exists to improve understanding.

Never entertainment.

---

# Philosophy

Move the camera.

Never the engineering.

Engineering systems possess mass.

Weight.

Purpose.

They should feel grounded.

Continuous spinning destroys this illusion.

---

# Camera Hierarchy

There are four categories of camera movement.

Level 1

Establish

Introduce the complete engineering system.

Level 2

Explore

Reveal subsystem relationships.

Level 3

Investigate

Focus on engineering details.

Level 4

Validate

Present simulation, testing and evidence.

Each camera movement belongs to one category.

---

# Camera Objectives

Every camera movement must answer exactly one question.

Examples

"What does the complete vehicle look like?"

"Where is the steering rack located?"

"How is the upper wishbone mounted?"

"What fails first under load?"

"What changed after topology optimisation?"

If no educational question exists,

remove the movement.

---

# Preferred Camera Motions

Orbit

Reveal proportions.

Pan

Compare systems.

Tilt

Reveal vertical relationships.

Dolly

Increase engineering detail.

Crane

Reveal architecture.

Reframe

Shift attention without changing context.

Never rotate endlessly.

Never drift continuously.

---

# Camera Anchors

Every project should define preset camera positions.

Hero

Engineering Overview

Packaging

Suspension

Steering

Powertrain

Telemetry

Manufacturing

Testing

Competition

Reflection

Camera movement should interpolate between anchors.

Never teleport.

---

# Camera Constraints

Maximum orbit

120°

Never 360°

Maximum zoom

Enough to inspect detail

Never clip geometry

Maximum tilt

Enough to reveal relationships

Never distort perspective

---

# Repository Rules

One camera move.

One engineering lesson.

Never surprise the visitor.

Camera movement must always increase understanding.

---
