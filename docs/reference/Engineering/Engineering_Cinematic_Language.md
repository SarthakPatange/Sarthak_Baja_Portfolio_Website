# Engineering Cinematic Language

Version: 1.0

Status: Repository Design Standard

Purpose

This document defines the cinematic language unique to this engineering portfolio.

Unlike the Apple reference documents, which analyse existing design philosophies, this document establishes an original framework for presenting engineering systems through interactive storytelling.

It serves as the bridge between industrial design principles and engineering communication.

The objective is not to impress visitors through visual effects.

The objective is to make complex engineering understandable, memorable and enjoyable.

The portfolio should not feel like a website.

It should feel like a guided engineering documentary.

---

# Core Philosophy

Engineering is inherently visual.

Most engineering portfolios reduce it to text and images.

This repository reverses that relationship.

Visual understanding comes first.

Technical explanation comes second.

Evidence comes third.

The visitor should understand the engineering before reading about it.

# Cinematic Principles

Engineering should never appear static.

However,

motion must communicate understanding.

Never movement for movement's sake.

Every transition should answer

"What should the visitor understand now?"

---

# Scene Philosophy

Every viewport is a scene.

Every scene has

one engineering objective

one visual subject

one dominant interaction

one educational outcome

No scene should attempt to explain multiple concepts simultaneously.

---

# Camera Philosophy

The visitor follows the camera.

The visitor should never need to search for important information.

# Engineering Storytelling

Do not describe engineering.

Demonstrate it.

Do not claim performance.

Validate it.

Do not claim optimization.

Show topology optimization.

Do not claim safety.

Show FEA.

Do not claim handling.

Show vehicle dynamics.

Evidence always outranks claims.

# Engineering Truth

If an engineering claim cannot be supported,

do not display it.

If validation does not exist,

describe limitations honestly.

Credibility always outweighs appearance.

# Repository Rules

One engineering lesson per viewport.

One subsystem at a time.

Camera teaches.

Motion explains.

Evidence validates.

Typography introduces.

Simulation educates.

Manufacturing proves.

Testing verifies.

Competition demonstrates.

Reflection teaches.

---

# Anti Patterns

Hero animations with no engineering.

Random spinning CAD.

Hover effects replacing explanation.

Large decorative gradients.

Continuous particle systems.

Gaming aesthetics.

Engineering dashboards everywhere.

Walls of specifications.

Marketing language.

Technology buzzwords.

Visual clutter.

---

# Definition of Success

Visitors should finish every project thinking

"I understand why these engineering decisions were made."

rather than

"That was an impressive animation."

Engineering understanding is the product.

Everything else supports it.
