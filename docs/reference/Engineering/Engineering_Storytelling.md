# Engineering Storytelling

Version: 1.0

Status: Repository Design Standard

Purpose

Engineering is often presented as a collection of results.

Drawings.

FEA.

CAD.

Specifications.

Manufacturing photographs.

Competition rankings.

This repository rejects that approach.

Visitors should never feel like they are reading documentation.

They should feel like they are discovering why engineering decisions were made.

The objective is understanding.

Not information.

---

# Storytelling Philosophy

Every engineering project is fundamentally a story.

Every engineering story begins with a problem.

Not with CAD.

Not with simulation.

Not with software.

The visitor should first understand

why

before

how.

# Why Before What

Never begin with

"We designed..."

Instead begin with

"The vehicle consistently understeered during tight corner entry."

Or

"The previous suspension generated excessive roll."

Or

"The steering geometry introduced bump steer."

Visitors understand problems.

Then they appreciate solutions.

---

# Engineering Decisions

Every engineering decision should answer

What changed?

Why?

What alternatives existed?

Why was this option selected?

What trade-offs were accepted?

Engineering is decision making.

Not modelling.

---

# Trade-Offs

Never present engineering as perfect.

Every improvement creates compromise.

Document

Mass

↓

Strength

↓

Cost

↓

Manufacturability

↓

Complexity

↓

Serviceability

↓

Reliability

Visitors trust honest engineering.

---

# Simulation

Simulation is not evidence.

Simulation predicts.

Testing validates.

Competition proves.

Never confuse these stages.

---

# Engineering Honesty

Never hide uncertainty.

If testing was incomplete,

say so.

If assumptions exist,

document them.

If compromises were necessary,

explain them.

Trust comes from transparency.

# Language

Prefer

"We selected..."

"We evaluated..."

"We validated..."

"We observed..."

"We improved..."

Avoid

"Best"

"Revolutionary"

"Incredible"

"Game-changing"

"World-class"

Engineering speaks through evidence.

Not adjectives.

---

# Numbers

Every important number should answer

Compared to what?

Under what conditions?

How was it measured?

Can it be reproduced?

Numbers without context have little value.

---

# Reflection

Every project ends with reflection.

What worked?

What failed?

What would change?

What surprised the team?

Reflection demonstrates maturity.

---

# Repository Rules

Every project begins with a problem.

Every decision has reasoning.

Every claim has evidence.

Every simulation has limitations.

Every result has context.

Every project ends with lessons learned.

Engineering should educate.

Not advertise.

---

# Anti Patterns

Tool lists

Software logos

Buzzwords

Specification dumping

Large feature lists

Unexplained graphs

Random CAD renders

Simulation screenshots without interpretation

Competition results without engineering context

Marketing language

Engineering theatre

---

# Definition of Success

The visitor should finish every project believing

"I understand why these engineering decisions were made."

not

"I saw many impressive engineering images."

Understanding is the product.

Engineering is the story.

Everything else supports it.
